# A Predictive-Prescriptive Framework for Compliance-Aware Time-Window Reallocation in Last-Mile Delivery

Last-mile delivery companies promise a delivery time window, but drivers often depart from the planned route. This paper treats that gap as manageable before it happens, not only measurable afterward. Using a public logistics dataset spanning two countries and 32 weeks, we find that drivers follow the planned stop sequence exactly on only 23.8 percent of routes, and 22 percent of deliveries arrive outside their promised window. We build two predictive models from planning-time information alone, one estimating reorder risk and the other arrival time, and feed them to a compliance-aware optimizer that reallocates each route’s existing time-window budget across stops without enlarging it. We formulate this allocation as a multiple-choice knapsack problem solved via constraint programming, addressing data leakage with a counterfactual-validity argument; results are read as retrospective extrapolation, not prospective deployment. Reallocation raises the modeled on-time delivery rate from 59.8 to 80.1 percent. Most of this 20-point gain comes from recentering and redistributing the budget: an equal split that ignores risk already reaches 80.0 percent. Risk-informed targeting adds a small but statistically significant 0.039 points, and solving the allocation exactly, over a greedy heuristic or genetic algorithm, adds a smaller but still significant 0.002-to-0.003 points while running far faster than the genetic algorithm. The contribution is a predictive-prescriptive framework, validated on real operational data, that reallocates a fixed time-window budget using planning-time behavioral predictions.
